How a simulation works

The same rhythm, mapped to each course's outcomes
1

A role and a real problem

You take a job title from the field and work a scenario built around the course outcomes, with a reference binder of the data, formulas, and definitions you would actually have on hand.

2

Calls that carry weight

Every decision moves a set of meters, and every choice tells you why it landed the way it did. Reflection checkpoints stop the action and ask you to put your reasoning in your own words.

3

Reasoning made submittable

You close with a read on how sound your calls were and a summary you can copy or download: scores, every reflection, and the full decision record, ready to paste into an assignment.

For instructors

No accounts, logins, or class codes needed

Students open the page in a browser, pick a scenario, and work through it. There is nothing to set up and no data leaves their computer. When they finish they copy or download a one-page summary with their scores, reflections, decision record, and time spent, and hand that in however you already collect work.

1

Fits a single class meeting

The 30-minute run is 8 decisions with two reflection checkpoints. In a 75-minute period that leaves room for about 10 minutes of framing, the run itself, and a solid 20 minutes of debrief while the decisions are still fresh.

2

Grade the reflections, not the score

The meters are feedback for the student. The graded thinking is in the reflections, where they explain in their own words why they made the calls they did. Every response appears verbatim in the summary.

3

Collect the summary

Students can copy the summary as plain text to paste straight into a discussion post or assignment box, or download it as a formatted file. Either way it carries their name, scores, reflections, and full decision record.

4

Compare paths in the debrief

The decision record shows exactly what each student chose. Asking two students who diverged on the same decision to defend their reasoning is usually a better discussion than reviewing the correct answers.

5

The reference binder is open during play

Each scenario carries its own binder of data tables, procedures, formulas, and definitions, reachable at any point in the run. Students are not being tested on recall.

6

Try it yourself first

Because there are no accounts, you can play a full scenario in half an hour and see exactly what your students will see, including the summary they will hand you.

Adding a simulation: each one is a single entry in the data file, listing the meters, an optional reference binder, the rounds of decisions with their reflection prompts, and the endings. Nothing in the engine needs to change to add a new course, a new scenario, or a build for another instructor or client.
